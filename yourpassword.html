    Why length matters more than complexity

    A 20-character lowercase password has more possible combinations than a 10-character mixed password. Every extra character multiplies the search space exponentially — length is the single most impactful factor in password strength.

    What is entropy?

    Entropy measures unpredictability in bits. Each bit doubles the guesses needed. A password with 60 bits requires 2⁶⁰ ≈ 1 quadrillion guesses. Modern GPUs try ~10 billion passwords/second — aim for 80+ bits for important accounts.

    Why P@ssw0rd is weak Weak

    Attackers already include common leet-speak substitutions (a→@, o→0, e→3) in their dictionaries. A password that looks complex but follows a predictable pattern provides far less security than it appears. The checker above detects these patterns in real time.

    The case for passphrases Recommended

    Four random words from the EFF list carry ~51 bits of entropy and are far easier to remember than Xk!9mQ2#. Six words gives ~77 bits — strong enough for most purposes. Ideal for master passwords.

    Why crypto.getRandomValues() matters

    The browser's Math.random() is not cryptographically secure — its output can be predicted. All generators here use crypto.getRandomValues(), which draws from the OS entropy pool and is resistant to prediction attacks.

    How k-anonymity protects your breach check

    When checking for breaches, this tool sends only the first 5 hex characters of your password's SHA-1 hash to HaveIBeenPwned. The server returns all hashes starting with those 5 chars — your full hash is matched locally. The server never sees your full hash or password.

    Always use two-factor authentication

    Even a strong password can be phished or leaked. 2FA means an attacker needs both your password and physical access to your device. Use an authenticator app (not SMS when possible) for important accounts.
